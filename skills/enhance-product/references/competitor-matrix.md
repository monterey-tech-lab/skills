# Competitor matrix

Mandatory in Step 1: verify who peers are and what they offer.

## Pick the set (3–5)

1. Start from the user’s named rival (if any) — do not stop there.
2. Search `"product" alternatives`, `"product" vs`, G2/Capterra category, Product Hunt / HN comparisons.
3. Classify each peer:
   - **Direct** — same primary job and buyer
   - **Adjacent** — different primary job, same budget or workflow (substitutes)
   - **Legacy / bundle** — incumbent suite feature (e.g. Confluence vs Notion wiki)
4. Drop pure hobby tools unless the subject is indie/hobby.
5. Cap at **5** in the main matrix; list extras under “also considered.”

**Completion criterion:** matrix has ≥3 peers (or an explicit “category has fewer” note) with filled cells below.

## Matrix columns (minimum)

| Column | What to record |
|---|---|
| Positioning | One-line as *they* claim it (vendor claim) |
| Core offering | Features/services that define the product |
| Pricing signal | Public tiers/prices; note currency and billing period; “custom” if sales-led |
| Packaging quirks | Who pays (makers vs all seats), free tier limits, feature gates |
| Strength vs subject | What they win |
| Weakness vs subject | Where subject or others win |

Optional columns when relevant: integrations, AI capabilities, compliance/self-host, target ICP.

## Fill rules

- Prefer pricing/features pages over blogs. Re-check numbers; label approximate.
- “We don’t know” is better than a guessed price — leave cell empty and mark **unknown**.
- Derive **gaps** from matrix rows: peer has X, subject lacks X, and VOC or category demand supports X.

## Incorrect vs correct
